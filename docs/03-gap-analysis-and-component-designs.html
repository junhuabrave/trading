Gap analysis and detailed designs for the remaining components

Document 3. What v0.1.0 has proven, what is missing between it and a broker that can take an order, and a design for each missing piece detailed enough to start coding against the schema and sequencer that already exist.

Hot path: latency-critical, deterministic, C++Cold path: correctness-critical, services, any language

Where we are

Updated for v0.2.0. The debt below is paid except where marked, and the simulator harness exists: a synthetic day runs from client order through risk, a stub router, a multi-session venue simulator and an OMS stand-in, and replays bit for bit with every decision, plan and checkpoint hash recomputed. Seven scenarios and drills run under make test against stored baselines. The table keeps the v0.1 view with the v0.2 changes noted per row.

Documents 1 and 2 describe the whole system. v0.1.0 delivers the foundation they said to build first: the schema and generators, the snapshot tooling, the sequencer core and the pre-trade risk engine, each with a replay-determinism test that passes. That is real, and it is the part that is hardest to retrofit. It is also roughly a tenth of the engineering. Nothing yet touches a client, a venue or a market-data feed, and no component runs outside a single test process.

Two things follow. First, the next increments should be the ones that turn the foundation into a system that can be exercised end to end in simulation, because that is what makes every later component cheap to test: the simulator and the OMS, then market data and the router. Second, the debt already visible in v0.1.0 should be paid now, while the code is small, rather than after the OMS has been built on top of it.

What competitive means now

Document 1 defined the position: not the fastest, but the most provable. This section makes that concrete against the three kinds of firm a new API-first broker is measured against, and turns each claim into something the code has to demonstrate.

Table stakes in 2026

  • T+1 settlement in the US, and UK/EU moving to it in 2027: the post-trade pipeline must allocate and affirm same day.
  • CAT reporting with full linkage between client order, child orders and executions. The causeSeq chain gives this natively; the extraction still has to be written and tested against the CAT specification.
  • Rule 15c3-5 market-access controls that are provably applied before any order leaves. The risk engine and the venue gateway's accept check are that proof; both need to exist.
  • Rule 605 execution-quality reporting under the amended rule, and 606 order-routing disclosures. Both are queries over the log once the OMS records NBBO at fill time.
  • Regulation NMS tick-size and access-fee changes from the 2024 amendments. The tick tables and fee schedules are already versioned data, not code; confirm the compliance dates in force with counsel because parts have been litigated.
  • FIX 4.4 plus a binary API, drop copy, cancel-on-disconnect, a kill switch and hot failover. Clients and clearing firms ask about all of these in due diligence.

The honest gap. None of the differentiators above is possible without the OMS, router, market data and venue gateways, which are the unglamorous middle of the system. The sections below design them so that the competitive claims fall out of ordinary operation rather than being bolted on later.

Debt in what exists

Found while reading and testing v0.1.0. Each is small now and expensive later. Fix in v0.2 before the OMS depends on any of it.

Risk engine

All items below are done in v0.2 except the money units, which wait for the OMS increment because nothing consumes aggregate fields yet. The v0.2 test also caught an overflow in the margin requirement (notional times basis points in 64-bit) that the wide arithmetic now covers.

  • Message-rate window is fixed, not sliding. The one-second bucket on seqTs lets an account send twice its limit across a boundary. Replace with ten 100 ms buckets per account (a uint16[10] ring plus a head index, 24 bytes) and reject when the sum exceeds the limit. Still deterministic, still no allocation.
  • Duplicate clOrdId key can collide. The session, clOrdId and account are XORed into one 64-bit key. Store the pair (sessionId, clOrdId) as a 96-bit key in an open-addressing table instead; the same table replaces clOrdIds_ and is reused by the OMS for cancel-by-origClOrdId.
  • No position at session start. The engine learns positions only from fills it sees, and learns accounts only from BuyingPowerUpdate. Consume the snapshot's accounts section at load(), and consume PositionSnapshot (600) and ExposureSnapshot (402) so a mid-day restart and an overnight position both start from truth.
  • Replace is not evaluated. ReplaceOrder (102) has no path through the engine although UnknownOrder and OrderNotLive reasons exist. Add evaluateReplace: quantity down releases commitment in place; quantity up or price change re-runs the credit and collar checks on the delta.
  • Duplicate-order heuristic. Document 1 asks for detection of the same account, symbol, side, quantity and price within a window. Add a per-account ring of the last 16 order fingerprints and a reason code DuplicateOrder.
  • SSR re-pricing. Rejecting a short at or below the bid is correct but loses the order. The OMS design below re-prices to bid plus one tick and marks the order; risk accepts the marked order if the price test passes.
  • Money aggregates. As the README says, int64 at 1e-8 caps a firm-wide sum at $92 billion. Schema v3 adds a MoneyC type at 1e-4 units for exposure and buying-power aggregates, appended as new fields with the old ones deprecated. Per-order fields stay at 1e-8.
  • Tables. The cancel path is dominated by unordered_map::erase. The OMS introduces an open-addressing table keyed by our 64-bit ids; the risk engine adopts the same type for open orders, locates and positions.

Sequencer

All done in v0.2 except the move to shared memory between processes, which is the platform increment.

  • Reader handler is a std::function. That is an indirect call per frame on the hot path. Make StreamReader::poll a template over the handler and keep a type-erased overload for tools.
  • Journal is one unbounded file. A day of market data is hundreds of gigabytes. Move to preallocated 1 GiB segments with a segment index, so recovery scans one segment and replay seeks by segment. This is also the shape Aeron Archive has, which keeps the swap open.
  • Checkpoint state hash is zero. The sequencer writes Checkpoint without a hash; engines' hashes are only compared inside the tests. Add a CheckpointAck message (id 13) that each engine sequences with its hash after a checkpoint, so divergence is detectable in production, not just in CI.
  • Frame flags. replayed and simulated are defined and never set. The reader sets replayed on journal refills; the simulator sets simulated on everything it injects. Compliance extraction uses both.
  • Single process. The ring is a C++ object, not a shared-memory segment. The platform section covers the move to Aeron IPC.

Schema and tooling

Done in v0.2: the schema additions, the Go emitter with a CI round trip, loginspect, the clang and libc++ sanitizer job, and the two fuzz targets. The benchmark gate is the v0.2 item still open (see benchmarks and guardrails).

  • Done in v0.2. Schema v3, all append-only: BookSnapshot (205), FeedStatus (207), MdSourceSwitch (15), ClockSync (14), CheckpointAck (13), VenueSessionStatus (110), ChildOrder.venueSessionIdx, LimitUpdate.dupWindowMs, VenueScorecard (308), RoutingProfileUpdate (309), the FeedRecord composite (the snapshot builder writes its section when the feed layer lands), ComponentRole.DropCopy, OrderFlags.ssrRepriced, and DuplicateOrder in reasons.csv. Multiple watermark messages per batch instead of a second bank, see the market-data section.
  • Market-data streams are not sequenced. Document 2 gave each market-data stream its own sequencer. The market-data section below replaces that with per-feed journals keyed by the venue's sequence number, and MdWatermark pairs become (feedId, venueSeq). The watermark tracker and the reader's gap refill keep their shape; only the identity changes.
  • Go emitter in sbegen.py, because the cold-path services are Go by the target profile and must decode the log. The TypeScript projection can wait for the web client.
  • A tools/loginspect utility: dump a journal by sequence range or template, follow a causeSeq chain, diff two journals. Every later component's debugging starts here.
  • CI: add a clang and libc++ build with AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer, a fuzz target each for the journal recovery scan and the snapshot loader (both parse files that could be corrupt), and the benchmark gates defined in benchmarks and guardrails.

Simulator harness sim/

Purpose
Run every hot-path binary, unchanged, against recorded or synthetic days with a deterministic clock, so that a component is tested by exact comparison before it exists in production. Document 1 called this the single biggest quality multiplier; it is still the toy inside test_seq.cpp.
Shape
  • Scenario is a log. A scenario is a journal of external events (client orders, feed packets as BookDelta/Trade, lender responses, operator actions) with originTs set and seq zero. Python produces them (sim/scenarios/*.py) or they are cut from a captured day. The driver feeds events in originTs order to the right stream's sequencer, advancing the FakeClock to each event's time.
  • VenueSim. One per venue id. Keeps an L2 book per symbol from the feed events. A child order arrives after VenueRecord.oneWayLatencyNs; an aggressive child crosses displayed liquidity in price-time order and emits VenueAck then ExecReports with liquidityFlag set; a passive child joins the queue at its level with queue position equal to the displayed quantity at arrival, and is filled as subsequent Trade events at that price consume the queue ahead of it. Rejects (bad tick, halted, unknown symbol) use the venue's reason codes. It exposes several sessions per venue, each with its own throttle, sequence and cancel-on-disconnect scope, and a drop-copy session that reports every execution independently. Cancel-on-disconnect, session drops and a port dying with live orders are scenario events.
  • FeedSim. Replays captured or synthetic feeds as separate A and B lines with a retransmit and snapshot service, at recorded timestamps, compressed time, or as fast as possible; drops, reorders or duplicates packets per line; and can present the same feed through two publishers so consumer failover is exercised.
  • ClientSim. Generates NewOrder flow: random (as test_risk does), replayed from a recorded client session, or adversarial (rate bursts, duplicate ids, cancels of unknown orders).
  • Harness. Wires one sequencer, journal and ring per stream in one process for CI; in multi-process mode uses the same Aeron IPC the production deployment uses. Starts each engine, drives the scenario, then runs the determinism checker: every engine's CheckpointAck hash must equal a second cold replay's hash, and must equal the stored baseline for that scenario.
Determinism
Sim and production differ only in who writes the external events. Everything an engine sees is sequenced; the simulated frame flag is set on injected events so nothing simulated can ever be mistaken for a real fill downstream.
Tests it enables
Golden runs: a recorded day per venue with stored hashes and decision counts, replayed in CI on every pull request. Property runs: random scenarios with invariants (no trade-through, child quantity never exceeds parent leaves, buying power never negative). Failure drills: sequencer restart, torn journal, feed gap, venue disconnect, kill switch mid-flight.
Size
About 4 000 lines of C++ and 1 000 of Python; five weeks. It is built first because the OMS and router are tested through it.

Order manager core/oms

Purpose
Own the lifecycle of every parent and child order, aggregate child fills to the parent, and produce the one client-facing ExecReport per event with the NBBO at that moment on it. The README lists it as the next component; the schema already carries its messages.
State
  • An order arena: 4 M slots of 256 bytes on huge pages, free list, no allocation after start. A slot holds the ids (order, client, parent, session), account and symbol, side, type, TIF, quantity, leaves, cumulative quantity and cumulative notional (average price is derived, never stored rounded), status, an intrusive child list, the accepted risk decision's sequence, the locate id, the venue and venue session that placed each child, and timestamps.
  • Three open-addressing tables sized at start: orderId to slot, (sessionId, clOrdId) to slot for cancel and replace by original client id, and childOrderId to slot. All 64-bit keys, linear probing, power-of-two capacity, tombstones compacted at end of day.
  • The fee table from the snapshot's fee section plus sequenced FeeScheduleUpdates, indexed by (venue, feeCode, liquidity) with tier thresholds.
  • Last Nbbo per symbol from the market-data streams, so each fill can record nbboBid/nbboAsk.
State machine
A table indexed by (status, event) giving the next status or Illegal. Statuses are the schema's OrdStatus; events are RiskAccept, RiskReject, ChildAck, ChildReject, PartialFill, Fill, CancelRequest, CancelAck, CancelReject, ReplaceRequest, ReplaceAck, Expire, Kill, TradeBust, TradeCorrect. An illegal transition is a logged Alert and a no-op, never a crash and never a guess. The table is data, so the exhaustive test enumerates every cell.
Flow
  1. NewOrder arrives: allocate slot, PendingNew. RiskDecision Accept: Live, emit OrderState. Reject: emit ExecReport with execType Rejected and the reason; free the slot at end of day, not immediately, so late messages resolve.
  2. The router's ChildOrders link to the parent. VenueAck moves the child to Live; VenueReject to Rejected and notifies the router to re-plan the remainder.
  3. A venue-level ExecReport from the gateway updates the child, then the parent's leaves, cumulative quantity and notional, then emits the parent-level ExecReport with fee computed from the table, liquidity flag and NBBO. execId is minted here; the venue's id is carried verbatim.
  4. CancelOrder: PendingCancel; cancels each live child on the session that placed it; Cancelled when the last child resolves. ReplaceOrder: quantity down applies in place to children proportionally; quantity up or a price change is a cancel of children plus a new route, and the delta goes back through risk with causeSeq pointing at the replace.
  5. Time in force: Day orders expire at the venue's close from the calendar, via a sequenced Timer; GTC orders are written to an open-orders section of the next snapshot and re-injected at SessionStart with the replayed flag.
  6. SSR: a short sale at or below the bid while SSR is active is re-priced to bid plus one tick, flagged in orderFlags, and sent through risk; the client sees the re-price on the acknowledgement.
  7. Kill switch: on KillSwitch every live order for the scope moves to PendingCancel and every child is cancelled through the gateways in one batch.
  8. Venue session down: every child on that session moves to PendingCancel awaiting the venue's confirmation, and the router is asked to re-plan the parents' remainders.
  9. Drop copy: the venue's drop-copy feed is folded as a second source of executions; an execution seen there and not from a session worker is a break, sequenced as an Alert and paged. The OMS also keeps a per-venue firm counter for message-to-trade and cancel ratios that apply across all sessions.
Determinism
All inputs are sequenced; NBBO is read from the market-data stream and each emitted batch carries an MdWatermark, exactly as the sequencer test does. No wall clock. The whole day is replayable and the client-visible reports regenerate byte for byte, which is how a dispute is settled.
Tests
Exhaustive (status, event) table test. Property test with random legal and illegal event sequences. Replay determinism. Fee computation against published schedule examples. A dispute drill: regenerate a day's client reports from the journal and compare to those sent.
Target
One microsecond per transition; four million parent orders a day on one core with headroom.
Size
About 3 000 lines; four weeks after the simulator exists.

Smart order router core/router

Purpose
Turn each accepted parent into child orders across venues that maximise expected net price, record the alternative it rejected, and release children so they land together.
Inputs
Live parents from the OMS; per-venue books and the NBBO from the shared-memory book view; VenueStatus; VenueSessionStatus for every session in each venue's pool; the fee table; a VenueScorecard (new message 308: venue, symbol or zero for all, fill rate, effective spread, post-fill reversion, latency p50 and p99, all in basis points or nanoseconds) sequenced by the TCA service; and a RoutingProfileUpdate (309) mapping a profile id to a strategy and its parameters.
Strategies
Each is a pure function from (parent, book views, scorecard, fees, profile) to a plan of up to eight children with venue, venue session, quantity, price, type, TIF, flags and release offset, plus the expected cost and the best rejected alternative. The initial library: Sweep (take displayed liquidity at or better than the limit across venues, cheapest net first, ISO flag when crossing protected quotes); DarkFirst (midpoint at ATSs with a time limit, then sweep); PassivePost (rest on the venue with the best rebate and fill rate, cross after a timer); MidPeg; and InternalizeFirst (ask the internaliser when the market maker quotes the symbol, then fall through).
Cost model
For each venue and liquidity type: expected net price = fill probability × (price adjusted by fee or rebate) + (1 − fill probability) × cost of the fallback, plus a reversion penalty from the scorecard. Fill probability comes from the scorecard per venue and liquidity type, updated daily by TCA. Session placement is part of the same decision: the least loaded session with throttle headroom, with symbol affinity where the venue wants it, and a saturated session enters as a cost rather than a wall. The strategy also declares the data quality it needs, and steps down to NBBO-only behaviour or pauses when the source selector reports less than Depth. The chosen plan and the runner-up are written to RouteDecision; that record is the best-execution evidence.
Release
ChildOrder.releaseTs = now + (slowest venue latency − this venue's latency) from VenueRecord.oneWayLatencyNs, refined by measured acks. The venue gateway holds the child until then. This stops a fast venue from printing before a slow one sees the order.
Re-planning
On a child reject, cancel or partial fill leaving quantity, the strategy is re-entered with the remaining quantity and the current books. Each child carries a strategyTag so TCA can attribute outcomes to the strategy step.
Regulation
Never trade through a protected quote without ISO; respect locked and crossed markets; honour the client's noInternalize and allowDark flags; the fee optimisation is logged so a best-ex committee can see when rebate capture would have cost price improvement.
Determinism
The router submits [MdWatermark, RouteDecision, ChildOrder…] as one atomic batch, the pattern the sequencer test already proves. Replay reconstructs the exact books it saw.
Tests
Scenario tests in the simulator with named market shapes (faster but thinner venue, rebate versus price, locked market, halt mid-sweep). Invariants: no trade-through, total child quantity never exceeds parent leaves, ISO only when allowed. Replay determinism. A 606 report generated from a simulated day.
Size
About 3 000 lines plus 400 per strategy; five weeks including the TCA feedback loop in Python.

Market data core/md

This section supersedes the sequenced market-data streams in document 2's log topology. The change: market data is not sequenced by us at all. A normalised message is identified by the venue's own sequence number. That one decision lets any number of redundant handlers produce identical streams, lets a consumer fail over between them without a gap or a duplicate, and lets the same arbitration logic run between lines at the bottom of the stack and between sources at the top.

Purpose
Turn every line of every feed into one gap-free, normalised, replayable stream per feed; let consumers choose and switch sources; build books and an NBBO from whichever sources are healthy; keep hardware timestamps and full capture throughout.
Identity
Every normalised message carries (feedId, venueSeq, index within packet). A feed is one venue protocol from one source at one site, declared in a new snapshot section: FeedRecord with feedId, venueId, protocol, kind (direct, SIP, vendor), site, its lines, and what it provides (depth, top of book, trades, status). Two decoders fed the same packets emit byte-identical messages, because the decoder is a pure function and the identity comes from the venue. Our sequencer is not involved and cannot be: two sequencers cannot agree on numbers, so a sequenced stream has exactly one publisher, which is the opposite of what redundancy needs.
Layers
  1. Line receiver. One per multicast group or TCP line: A, B, retransmit, snapshot, a second site's line, a vendor line. Reads packets, stamps NIC hardware time into originTs, writes raw capture. Knows nothing about the protocol.
  2. Line arbitrator. One per feed. Merges its lines by venue sequence number, first arrival wins; detects gaps; requests retransmit (MoldUDP64 for ITCH) or a snapshot (Glimpse) and splices the recovery in order; publishes FeedStatus (healthy, recovering, stale, down) with each line's latency against its sibling, which is the first line-quality metric. Protocol-agnostic except for where the sequence number sits in the packet.
  3. Decoder. One per protocol: ITCH 5.0, the UTP and CTA SIPs, PITCH, Pillar. A pure function from packet to normalised messages, with the order-reference table ITCH needs (open addressing, 226 entries) as its only state. Emits BookDelta with absolute quantity per level, Trade, SymbolStatus and Imbalance, with endOfPacket on the last message of a packet.
  4. Source selector. One per symbol range. Two jobs. Dedupe identical sources (two decoder instances, two sites) by identity, taking whichever arrives first, exactly as the arbitrator does for lines. And choose between non-identical sources (direct, SIP, vendor) by policy when the preferred one is stale or gapped, publishing the quality level it currently serves: Depth, TopOfBook, TradesOnly, None.
  5. Book builder. One per symbol range. Per symbol and venue, flat ladders of 64 levels indexed by tick from a base price, re-based on drift; a consolidated view; an NBBO from direct feeds with the SIP's carried alongside in Nbbo. Exposed through a shared-memory segment with a seqlock per symbol: a 128-byte top-of-book record for the router and market maker, full ladders for depth consumers. Publishes BookSnapshot per symbol every few seconds for late joiners.
Journals and replay
The arbitrator writes the gap-free packet stream per feed. The decoder's output is reproducible from it, so the normalised journal per feed is a cache, kept because replaying normalised messages is an order of magnitude cheaper than re-decoding. Both are segmented files keyed by venue sequence, so replay to a watermark is a seek. The simulator's FeedSim reads either.
Consumer failover
A consumer subscribes to a logical feed, not to a publisher. It keeps a high-water (feedId, venueSeq); on switching publishers it discards anything at or below it and refills any gap from any publisher's journal. This is StreamReader's gap logic with the identity changed, so the existing code generalises. Switching between identical sources is silent. Switching between non-identical sources changes what an engine sees, so the engine sequences MdSourceSwitch (feed from, feed to, quality level) on the core stream and replay reproduces it.
Degradation is explicit
The SIP is not a substitute for a direct feed. Direct gives per-venue depth; the SIP gives top-of-book quotes and trades. Consumers declare what they need. The risk engine needs reference prices and runs on TradesOnly. The market maker needs Depth and pulls quotes without it. The router's Sweep and PassivePost need Depth and fall back to NBBO-only behaviour, or pause, when the selector reports TopOfBook. A strategy never runs on data that looks the same and is not.
Determinism
MdWatermark records (feedId, venueSeq) pairs; the existing layout fits. Block lengths are frozen by the compatibility checker, so rather than a second bank of pairs, an engine that consumes more than eight feeds sequences several watermarks in the same batch, all before the decision; the tracker (v0.2) fills them and the replayer merges them. Each engine's own interleaving of feeds is what its watermark records, so two engines may see an NBBO flip a few microseconds apart in a different order; each is exactly reproducible from its own watermarks, which is what replay and best-execution evidence require.
Deployment
Decoupled components are not separate processes. Each shared-memory hop costs a few hundred nanoseconds against a book-update budget under a microsecond. Layers 1 to 3 are library components with fixed interfaces composed into one pinned process per feed; layers 4 and 5 compose into one process per symbol range; the capture writer is a separate thread. The same components run as separate processes in the simulator when a failure between them is being injected. Redundancy is two feed processes per feed on different hosts or sites, both publishing the same logical feed.
Tests
Decoder golden tests against published ITCH sample days. Arbitrator drills in FeedSim: dropped, reordered and duplicated packets on one line, then both, then a retransmit that itself gaps. Selector drills: primary publisher killed mid-packet; direct feed stalled so the SIP takes over and the MdSourceSwitch appears on the core stream; direct returns. Book invariants. Throughput of three million messages per second per feed process; packet to published delta under two microseconds at the median; publisher switch with zero gap and zero duplicate over a million messages.
Size
Receiver and arbitrator 2 000 lines, shared; 2 000 per decoder; selector 1 500; book builder 2 000; capture and journals 1 000. Twelve weeks for ITCH plus the SIP end to end with redundancy. Vendor SDK for the long tail in year one, wrapped as a decoder so it plugs into layer 4.

Venue gateways core/venue

Venues limit each session: a message throttle per port, one login per port at a time, sometimes an open-order cap, and cancel-on-disconnect scoped to the session. One session per venue therefore caps the firm's throughput and makes one port the entire failure domain. The gateway is a pool of session workers with placement decided upstream and drop copy as the safety net.

Session worker
One per venue port: its own socket, login, sequence numbers, throttle budget (a token bucket at the venue's published rate) and cancel-on-disconnect scope. Pinned core, kernel-bypass socket. It consumes the core stream and acts only on children addressed to its session, holds each until releaseTs, and publishes VenueSessionStatus (up, down, throttle used, open orders, queue depth, ack latency) every few hundred milliseconds and on any change. A host runs as many workers as it has cores for. Workers for the busiest venue sit on the sequencer's host; the rest run on gateway hosts fed over Aeron multicast.
Placement
The router chooses the session as part of choosing the venue and records it in ChildOrder.venueSessionIdx (schema v3). VenueRecord.sessionIds already declares the pool per venue. Policy: the least loaded session with headroom under its throttle, with symbol affinity where the venue's self-trade prevention or ordering rules want it. Session load is a term in the router's cost model, so a saturated port becomes a slightly worse choice rather than a wall. Because session status is sequenced, placement is deterministic and replays exactly. There is no dispatcher process and no extra hop.
Affinity
A cancel or replace goes on the session that placed the order, because tokens are session-scoped at most venues. The OMS stores the session in the child slot and stamps it on every cancel and replace, so a worker's filter is the same for the order's whole life.
Back-pressure
A worker whose throttle would be exceeded queues and delays; it never drops. It publishes its queue depth and the router stops placing there until it clears. An accepted child is never silently lost.
Structural accept check
Each worker keeps a bitset of parent order ids seen in an accepting RiskDecision; a child whose parent is absent is dropped with a sequenced Alert. One bit test, and the enforcement document 1 promised.
Ids
The venue order token is minted from childOrderId so the reverse map on an ack is arithmetic; the venue's execution id is carried verbatim in venueExecId for clearing and CAT.
Failure domain
A port dying takes only its orders. The worker sequences VenueSessionStatus down; the OMS moves that session's children to pending cancel, awaiting the venue's confirmation; the router re-plans the remainders on surviving sessions. A standby worker on a second host logs in once the venue confirms the first login is gone, resyncs from the venue's resend, and takes the session. Session state is journaled so a restart resumes rather than resets.
Drop copy
The venue's drop-copy session is consumed as an independent source into the OMS. Any execution seen on drop copy that no worker reported is a break and pages immediately. With one session this is a nicety; with twenty it is what makes the pool safe.
Firm-level limits
Message-to-trade ratios, cancel fees and order-to-execution thresholds apply across all ports. The OMS keeps a per-venue firm counter and the router treats the marginal cost as a term. Ports are procured from the exchange with cost and lead time in weeks, so the go-live plan states ports per venue and the order in which they are certified.
Protocols
OUCH 5.0 over SoupBinTCP first, then Pillar binary, BOE, and FIX 4.2 for ATSs and wholesalers. Each is an encoder and decoder plugged into the same worker. The simulator twin speaks the real protocol to VenueSim over a real session, so the code path in CI is the production one.
Tests
Codec round trips against the specifications' byte examples. Session drills: disconnect mid-order, sequence gap, duplicate ack, cancel-on-disconnect, a port dying with live orders and the remainder re-routing. Throttle drill: a burst above the rate, then queueing, status publication and router avoidance. Drop-copy break injection. The property "never sends without an accept". Placement determinism under replay. Child sequence to wire under 1.5 microseconds.
Size
Worker framework and placement 2 500 lines; 2 000 per protocol; six weeks for OUCH including a multi-session VenueSim, three per subsequent protocol.

Client gateways core/gateway

Purpose
Accept orders from clients, authenticate, normalise to internal messages, and deliver execution reports with the client's identifiers intact.
Binary protocol
The internal SBE frames over TCP with a 16-byte session envelope (session id, client sequence, HMAC or the mTLS session binding). The gateway resolves ticker to symbolIdx through the snapshot's ticker index, account from the session, stamps originTs from the NIC timestamp, and sequences the NewOrder. Outbound, it filters the core stream's ExecReports by session. Per-session inbound and outbound sequence numbers are journaled so a reconnect resumes with a resend of anything unacknowledged.
FIX 4.4
License a low-latency C++ engine or build one; document 1 makes the case for licensing. Either way the FIX session sits in its own thread, translates to the same internal messages, and the drop-copy sessions for clients and for compliance are generated from the log so they cannot disagree with the book.
Throttles
Message rate per session, open-order count per session and cancel-to-fill ratio warnings. These are protective; the risk engine is where the controls live.
Tests
Fuzzing of the inbound decoder. Reconnect drills with in-flight orders. A FIX conformance suite. Binary gateway ingress-to-sequenced under two microseconds.
Size
Binary gateway about 2 000 lines, three weeks. FIX four weeks with a licensed engine, twelve or more without.

Position keeper core/poskeeper services/ledger

Hot view
Preallocated arrays keyed by (account, symbol) through an open-addressing table: quantity, cumulative cost, borrowed quantity, and per-account gross and net notional. Folds every parent-level ExecReport fill. Publishes PositionSnapshot (600) per changed position at each checkpoint and ExposureSnapshot (402) per account, which is what the risk engine's start-of-session load consumes. Exposed through shared memory with a seqlock for the market maker and margin engine. Applies CorporateAction at effectiveSeq (the split fixture already exists), so every consumer adjusts at the same sequence number.
Cold ledger
A Go service over Postgres, event-sourced from the same log: lots (LotUpdate), FIFO and specific-identification for tax, average cost for the desk view, realised and unrealised P&L, fees, marks, and the full history. Position at any sequence number is a query. End-of-day snapshot; restart replays from the last one.
Reconciliation
The clearing firm's position and cash files are loaded daily and diffed against the ledger. Every break becomes a ticket with an owner and a deadline; the ageing report is the operations dashboard. This is the first cold-path service to build because self-clearing and every examination depend on it.
Tests
Fold determinism between hot view and ledger (same log, same positions). Corporate-action tests for split, reverse split, symbol change and cash dividend. Lot accounting property tests. A break-detection test with a deliberately wrong clearing file.
Size
Hot view 1 500 lines, two weeks. Ledger and reconciliation 5 000 lines of Go, six weeks.

Platform and failover core/seq ops/

Transport
Replace the in-process BroadcastRing with Aeron IPC for components on one host and Aeron UDP multicast to the standby host, behind the existing Sequencer and StreamReader interfaces. The ordering logic, batching, watermarks and checkpoints do not change; that was the point of the interfaces. Keep our journal with segment rotation as the durable record, or adopt Aeron Archive for it once the multi-host replay path is exercised. The decision is a deployment one and is left open below.
Standby
A standby sequencer consumes the primary's fan-out and journals independently. Promotion: the control plane sequences nothing; it stops the primary's publication, the standby verifies its journal tail against the last CheckpointAcks, and resumes from its last durable sequence. Any frame the primary accepted but the standby never received is lost by design and is visible as a gap to every consumer, which is why gateways only acknowledge clients after the frame is on the fan-out. The procedure is drilled monthly, not merely written.
Engine restart
An engine loads the snapshot, verifies the hash against SessionStart, applies from its last checkpoint through the reader's journal refill, compares its hash to the CheckpointAck it wrote, and only then reports Live in ComponentStatus. Target under thirty seconds at ten million core messages a day.
Hot-path hygiene
Isolated cores, huge pages, NUMA-local memory, no syscalls on trading threads, logging through a lock-free ring drained elsewhere, OpenOnload on the NICs. A latency histogram per stage is written to the telemetry stream as LatencySample every second; a Prometheus exporter and Grafana dashboards read it; alerts are on the 99.9th percentile.
Tracing
A tools/trace command follows the causeSeq chain for an order and renders the waterfall with real timestamps, exporting OpenTelemetry spans. Determinism as a metric: a divergence between any engine's CheckpointAck and the standby's is a page.
Security
Mutual TLS or dedicated lines for client sessions; keys in an HSM once the firm is regulated; signed configuration; four-eyes approval on any LimitUpdate or KillSwitch release through the control plane; an immutable audit of operator actions written as sequenced messages so it is in the same log as their effect.
Tests
Two-process gap refill and failover drills in CI. A tail-latency gate per stage against a stored baseline. A chaos scenario in the simulator that kills the primary mid-batch and checks that no accepted order is lost and no unaccepted order is sent.
Size
Aeron binding and segmented journal 2 500 lines, four weeks. Standby and promotion 1 500 lines plus runbooks, three weeks. Telemetry and trace tooling 2 000 lines, three weeks.

Market maker and internaliser core/mm

Purpose
Quote continuously in a chosen universe, internalise client flow with logged price improvement, and keep inventory near flat under hard limits. Phase three in document 1's roadmap; designed here so the router's InternalizeFirst step and the position keeper's shared view are built with it in mind.
Engine
One engine per market-data stream (a symbol range), implementing the strategy interface from document 2. Fair value per symbol is a microprice from the top-of-book imbalance adjusted by short-horizon signals delivered as Signal messages: trade-flow imbalance, a correlated ETF move, realised volatility. Quote placement follows an inventory-aware model: reservation price shifted against inventory, spread widened with volatility and hedging cost, both bounded by ModelParams fitted offline and sequenced, so the live engine is arithmetic only. Quotes go out as QuoteIntent; the venue gateway maps them to the venue's quote or replace messages. Adverse-selection defence pulls or widens on VenueStatus, scheduled news windows from the calendar, and a ToxicityScore per counterparty class from the cold path.
Inventory
InventoryLimit per symbol, sector and firm is hard: a breach flattens through the router with a liquidation strategy tag, never by widening and hoping. Position comes from the position keeper's shared view at the sequence number of the last fill.
Internaliser
When a client NewOrder arrives in a quoted symbol and the client allows it, the internaliser checks the engine's live quote against the NBBO, and fills the client at a price that improves the NBBO by at least the configured minimum, in whole increments, up to the quoted size. It sequences InternalizeDecision with the improvement in money terms and an ExecReport with liquidity Internalized; otherwise it declines with a reason and the router continues. The conflict of interest is real, so every decision is on the log and the daily compliance job checks that improvement was delivered on every internalised fill.
Compliance
The bona fide market-making exemption from locates is used only while the engine is quoting continuously in the symbol; the compliance service verifies from the log each day and revokes the flag when the criteria fail.
Tests
Quoting replay against recorded days with stored intents; inventory limit breach drills; internaliser price-improvement invariant over random flow; toxicity response scenarios.
Size
Engine 4 000 lines, internaliser 1 000, research pipeline 3 000 lines of Python; twelve weeks with a quantitative researcher.

Stock loan services/stockloan

Purpose
Locates that name a source, an easy-to-borrow list that is true, borrow booking on every short execution, and the loan lifecycle without creating a fail. The messages exist and the risk engine already consumes them; the service that writes them does not.
Availability ledger
Postgres, per symbol: sources (firm box, client margin inventory eligible for rehypothecation with per-account limits, external lenders with offered quantity and rate, agent lender programmes) and reservations (locates granted and unused, borrows booked). Available quantity is a derived query, never a cached number.
Locate engine
The fast part, in Go with an in-memory copy of the ledger rebuilt from its own event log: a LocateRequest is answered with LocateGranted (with sourceId, quantity, rate, expiry at end of day and a reservation id) or LocateDenied within one millisecond. Partial grants are allowed and flagged. Each morning the ETB list is regenerated from verified availability and published as ListUpdates; an intraday borrow failure removes the symbol from the list immediately. There is no assume-available path.
Lifecycle
A short-sale ExecReport converts the reservation into a borrow request to the source; confirmation sequences BorrowBooked with rate and collateral terms; failure sequences BorrowFailed, alerts the desk and starts the buy-in workflow before settlement. Recalls search for replacement supply then force a buy-in by the deadline. Collateral is marked daily; RateUpdates feed research and the market maker's risk inputs. Fails-to-deliver run a Reg SHO close-out clock per position, and threshold securities are handled through the list.
Integration
Lender platforms and the clearing firm's stock-loan service through adapters; the ledger never trusts an external quantity until a borrow confirms.
Tests
Locate accounting to the unit (granted, consumed, returned on cancel, expired) matching the risk engine's view exactly. Borrow-failure drill removing a symbol from ETB and rejecting the next short. Recall and buy-in timelines. Reg SHO close-out clock property tests.
Size
About 8 000 lines of Go; ten weeks plus lender integrations.

Margin engine services/margin

Design
A Go rules service over the position keeper's view: Reg T initial and maintenance for US equities, house rules above them, concentration and hard-to-borrow add-ons per marginClass, portfolio margin later. Recomputes per account on a timer of seconds and on a large mark move, and sequences BuyingPowerUpdate, which the risk engine already treats as a full re-base of its incremental figure. Calls are MarginCall workflow objects with deadlines and escalation; forced liquidation is a NewOrder with a liquidation strategy id through the normal risk and router path, so it is auditable like any other order.
Tests
Worked Reg T examples from the rule; a drift test that the incremental buying power in the risk engine and the full recompute never differ by more than the fills in flight; call and liquidation timelines in the simulator.
Size
About 4 000 lines; six weeks. Portfolio margin is a separate project.

Post-trade, cash ledger and clearing services/posttrade

Design
Trade capture from the log; allocation of institutional blocks to sub-accounts by FIX allocation or the client's post-trade platform; a clearing-firm adapter that produces the trade, position and cash files and consumes the firm's breaks; a double-entry cash ledger per account and currency where every movement references a log event or an approved manual entry; corporate-action cash processing, dividends, interest on cash and margin, fee accruals, month-end close. The adapter boundary is drawn so self-clearing later replaces the adapter, not the pipeline.
T+1
Allocations and affirmations complete on trade date; the end-of-day job refuses to close with an unaffirmed institutional trade and pages operations.
Tests
Ledger invariants (every account balances, every entry has a cause). A full-day simulated flow through allocation to the clearing files, diffed against a stored baseline. Reconciliation break drills.

Compliance and reporting services/compliance

CAT
An extraction from the log: every order event with the linkages CAT requires, derived from causeSeq and the parent and child ids; the firm's and venues' identifiers carried verbatim. Submission through a reporting agent. Validated against the CAT specification's test cases and against the venue's own reported events.
Best execution
Per-order TCA against the NBBO at arrival and at each child release, computed from ExecReport.nbboBid/nbboAsk and RouteDecision; quarterly 605 under the amended rule and 606 reports; best-ex committee packs. The same TCA computes the venue scorecards that go back to the router as VenueScorecard, closing the loop.
Reg SHO

Surveillance
Buy a recognised package in year one; feed it from the log over Kafka or NATS with the Go codec. Build the one check that matters most for a broker with its own market maker: front-running of client orders by the market maker, which the log makes provable by ordering.
Retention
Daily journals and captures written once to immutable storage for the required period, with a retrieval drill each quarter.
Size
CAT and best-ex 6 000 lines of Go and Python; eight weeks. The surveillance feed two weeks.

Control plane
Configuration as code, versioned and signed. The component table (already in the snapshot) is its registry. Benchmarks and guardrails

Every latency claim in this document is worthless unless it is measured the same way in CI and in production, against a baseline, with a gate that fails the build. v0.1.0 has three micro-benchmarks and no gate. This section defines the measurement at three levels and the guardrails that keep numbers from drifting.

Three levels

Component
A micro-benchmark per hot-path component, in isolation, on synthetic input: decode and dispatch, risk evaluate, OMS transition, router plan, book update, decoder per packet, gateway encode, sequencer stamp and journal, ring hand-off. Each reports p50, p99, p99.9 and maximum over at least a million operations after warm-up, plus allocations and instructions retired. The existing bench.cpp, bench_seq.cpp and bench_risk.cpp are the first three; every component design above adds its own before it merges.
Front to back
Per-stage latency along a real order's path, measured from the log itself in the simulator: originTs to seqTs for ingress, then each causeSeq hop (order to risk decision, decision to route, route to child, child to venue ack, ack to client report) as the difference of the two seqTs values. No instrumentation in the engines; the header carries it. The same query runs over the production journal, so CI and production numbers are directly comparable stage by stage.
End to end
Two wire-to-wire figures. Agency: client NIC timestamp to child on the venue wire, with the real client and venue protocols talking to the simulator over real sockets. Tick to trade: feed packet NIC timestamp to child on the wire for a strategy that reacts to it. Both measured in the simulator on the pinned benchmark host, and in production from NIC timestamps at the gateways.

Budget and gates

The budget is document 1's latency table. The gate compares each run to a stored baseline for the host class and fails on regression, not on absolute value, so a slow CI machine does not hide a real regression and a fast one does not excuse it.

Guardrails that are not latency

  • No allocation after start-up. Test builds replace the global allocator with one that counts; every hot-path test asserts zero allocations after warm-up. A component that allocates on the order path fails its own test, not a review.
  • No syscalls on trading threads. A CI drill runs each engine under a syscall tracer for a simulated minute and fails on any call from the pinned thread other than the expected ring and timer operations.
  • Determinism. Every golden run compares state hashes; any divergence fails. Two builds of the same commit with different compilers must hash identically on the same scenario, which catches uninitialised memory and iteration-order dependence.
  • Queue depth. Ring lag per reader and journal write latency are sampled into the telemetry stream; a reader more than a quarter ring behind at any point in a golden run fails it.
  • Binary size and layout. The generated codec's static_asserts already freeze offsets; add a check that hot structs (order slot, book top record, account record) stay within their cache-line budget.
  • Coverage of reasons. Every reason code in reasons.csv must be produced by at least one test, as the risk test does today. A code nobody can trigger is either dead or untested.

Mechanics

  • tools/bench runs every benchmark, emits one JSON document per run with host class, commit, CPU model and kernel, and compares against bench/baselines/<host-class>.json. Pull requests run the component and front-to-back levels on a dedicated pinned runner, never a shared virtual machine, because noise on shared hardware is larger than the regressions being caught. Nightly runs add the end-to-end level over a full recorded day.
  • Baselines move only by an explicit commit that updates the file with the reason, reviewed like a schema change. Improvements lower the baseline the same way, so a later regression back to the old number is caught.
  • Production telemetry (LatencySample per stage) feeds the same dashboards with the same stage names, so the p99.9 a client is shown and the p99.9 the build gate enforces are one number.
  • A benchmark that cannot run, for example because the pinned runner is down, fails the build rather than being skipped. Skipped gates are how numbers drift.

Open decisions and risks

  • Aeron or our own transport. Aeron gives IPC, multicast and archive for free and is proven; our ring and journal are two thousand lines we fully understand. Recommendation: Aeron for transport now, our journal until Aeron Archive's replay is exercised across hosts in v0.4, then decide. The interfaces make the choice reversible.
  • FIX engine licence. Four weeks with a licensed engine against twelve or more without, and correctness under resend and gap scenarios is where in-house engines fail. Recommendation: license, and keep the binary protocol as the latency product.
  • Go for the cold path. The target profile says Go; nothing in the repo is Go yet and the emitter does not exist. Decide before the ledger starts, because it is the first cold service. Python would be faster to write and slower to operate.
  • Money units. Schema v3 is the moment to move aggregates to 1e-4 units. Doing it later means a second migration of every cold-path table.
  • Venue set and ports at go-live. Nasdaq, NYSE and Cboe cover the lit majority, and each needs a stated number of ports procured and certified in advance; the ATS list determines the DarkFirst strategy's value and each needs a FIX session and certification. Pick three ATSs early because their queues are long.
  • Market maker placement. Same process as the router per symbol range, or its own. Same process saves a hop on internalisation; separate is easier to kill. Recommendation: separate process, same host, shared-memory book view; the hop is under a microsecond.
  • Regulatory dates. Rule 605 amendments, the Regulation NMS tick and fee changes, and T+1 in the UK and EU have compliance dates that have moved and may move again. The tick tables, fee schedules and calendars are data so the code does not care; the compliance calendar does. Confirm with counsel before each phase gate.
  • The team. Everything above assumes document 1's staffing. With one or two engineers the order stays the same and the dates roughly double; the simulator and OMS are still the right next step because they are what make the rest cheap.
